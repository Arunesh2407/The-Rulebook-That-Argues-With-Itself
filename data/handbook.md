# University Academic Handbook

## 1. General Academic Regulations

The University Academic Handbook is the controlling statement of ordinary academic administration for undergraduate, graduate, certificate, exchange, and non-degree students enrolled in credit-bearing instruction. It establishes minimum expectations for registration, attendance, assessment, integrity, progression, grievance handling, and the custody of academic records.

Students are responsible for maintaining current contact information, consulting the academic calendar, reviewing course outlines, and preserving copies of submissions, approvals, medical validations, fee receipts, and official notices. The registrar may rely on institutional records unless a student provides timely documentary evidence demonstrating an error.

The University administers general academic regulation as an academic governance matter rather than as a private arrangement between a student and a single member of staff. Accordingly, the controlling standard is the published rule, the contemporaneous record maintained by the registrar, and the documented exercise of authority by the office charged with implementing the rule. Students are expected to read the applicable regulation before registration and again when circumstances arise that may affect enrollment, assessment, progression, or standing. Administrative convenience, informal advice, or prior tolerance of a different practice does not create an entitlement unless the regulation expressly recognizes such discretion.

For purposes of general academic regulation, notice is effective when sent to the student's official university account or posted in the approved learning management system for the course or program. A student who relies on a private message, a conversation in a corridor, or an unsigned departmental note assumes the risk that the information is incomplete. Offices may correct clerical errors, but correction of a record does not excuse missed deadlines unless the student can show that the error prevented timely compliance despite reasonable diligence. The registrar may require supporting material in original form whenever authenticity is material to the decision.

The ordinary remedy under general academic regulation is prospective compliance, not retroactive substitution of a preferred academic outcome. Where a request is allowed, the student must identify the specific course, term, instructor, assessment, date, and regulation relied upon. General statements of hardship, disagreement with a mark, or dissatisfaction with advising are insufficient unless tied to a procedural right recognized by the handbook. Decisions must be reasoned, recorded, and retained for audit so that similarly situated students receive consistent treatment across schools, departments, and modes of delivery.

Nothing in the administration of general academic regulation prevents an academic unit from adopting a higher standard where accreditation, clinical placement, laboratory safety, or professional licensure requires it. A higher standard must be announced before the relevant academic activity begins and must be applied uniformly to all students in the affected cohort. A lower standard is available only when this handbook expressly permits a lower standard and identifies the official empowered to approve it. Silence in a syllabus, catalog note, or advising worksheet shall not be read as permission to depart from university policy.

## 2. Attendance Requirements

Attendance is an academic requirement because courses include discussion, supervised practice, laboratory performance, collaborative critique, clinical preparation, and other forms of learning that cannot be reproduced solely by reading materials after the fact. Each instructor shall publish the method by which attendance is recorded and shall submit the verified attendance record when requested by the registrar.

Authorized participation in University representation, documented disability accommodation, religious observance recorded in advance, and verified emergency response service may justify administrative notation, but such notation does not itself award attendance for a session in which the student did not participate.

## 2.4 Final Examination Attendance Eligibility

No student whose verified attendance in a course is below seventy-five percent (75%) may sit for the final examination in that course, and no faculty member, advisor, chair, or committee has discretion to reduce, excuse, round, or otherwise waive this minimum examination eligibility floor.

The registrar shall determine examination eligibility from the certified attendance record after the final scheduled instructional meeting and before the examination period opens. A student declared ineligible may request correction of an arithmetic or identity error, but the correction process is limited to the accuracy of the attendance record and shall not consider merit, need, travel cost, expected graduation date, or the student's performance in other components of the course.

## 3. Continuous Assessment

Continuous assessment consists of quizzes, laboratory notebooks, studios, clinical demonstrations, field reports, presentations, portfolios, discussion contributions, midterm examinations, and other scheduled academic work completed during the teaching term. The purpose is to provide regular evidence of learning and to reduce reliance on a single terminal assessment.

Each course outline shall identify the components, weights, due dates, late submission rules, and minimum performance conditions for continuous assessment. Alterations after the add-drop period require written notice to the enrolled class and may not disadvantage a student who reasonably relied on the original schedule.

Prohibited conduct includes plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, fabrication of data, contract cheating, impersonation, misuse of artificial intelligence contrary to course instructions, possession of unauthorized materials in an assessment, and interference with another student's academic work.

Sanctions may include warning, grade reduction, failure of an assessment, failure of a course, suspension, expulsion, transcript notation, loss of honors eligibility, or restriction from placements and representative activities. The sanction shall reflect intent, prior history, educational impact, and the seriousness of the breach.

## 4.1 Authority for Prerequisite Waivers

Only the Dean of Academic Affairs has authority to waive a published prerequisite course, and recommendations from advisors, instructors, program coordinators, or department heads are advisory only until the Dean issues a written approval.

A request for waiver must include the student's transcript, the course description for any proposed equivalent study, evidence of relevant professional or laboratory experience where applicable, and a statement explaining how the student's preparation satisfies the learning outcomes protected by the prerequisite. Enrollment before written approval is at the student's risk and may be administratively reversed.

The University administers prerequisite waiver authority as an academic governance matter rather than as a private arrangement between a student and a single member of staff. ## 5. Prerequisite Waivers

A waiver is exceptional and does not imply that the waived course is unnecessary for degree completion unless a separate substitution or credit decision is issued.

Students seeking waivers should apply before registration opens for the term in which the advanced course will be attempted. Late applications may be considered only when the facts supporting the request were not reasonably known earlier.

## 5.2 Instructor Adjustment of Examination Eligibility

An individual course instructor may lower the final examination eligibility threshold to sixty-five percent (65%) for a student who has demonstrated satisfactory continuous evaluation performance, timely completion of formative work, and responsible engagement with required academic support.

The instructor's adjustment must be recorded before the examination list is finalized and must identify the continuous evaluation evidence relied upon. Adjustments are student-specific, course-specific, and term-specific. They do not amend the attendance record, create a general departmental practice, or bind other instructors in the same subject area.

The University administers instructor adjustment of examination eligibility as an academic governance matter rather than as a private arrangement between a student and a single member of staff. ## 6.1 Medical Excuse Validation

Medical excuses must be validated by the Campus Health Center within seven business days following the student's return to classes, and the validation must identify the period during which attendance or assessed work was medically impracticable.

Campus Health Center validation confirms that the documentation is medically plausible and temporally connected to the affected academic obligation. It does not compel an instructor to award a particular grade unless the applicable course rule or assessment policy provides that consequence.

The University administers appeals records and departmental determinations as an academic governance matter rather than as a private arrangement between a student and a single member of staff. Accordingly, the controlling standard is the published rule, the contemporaneous record maintained by the registrar, and the documented exercise of authority by the office charged with implementing the rule. Students are expected to read the applicable regulation before registration and again when circumstances arise that may affect enrollment, assessment, progression, or standing. Administrative convenience, informal advice, or prior tolerance of a different practice does not create an entitlement unless the regulation expressly recognizes such discretion.

For purposes of appeals records and departmental determinations, notice is effective when sent to the student's official university account or posted in the approved learning management system for the course or program. A student who relies on a private message, a conversation in a corridor, or an unsigned departmental note assumes the risk that the information is incomplete. Offices may correct clerical errors, but correction of a record does not excuse missed deadlines unless the student can show that the error prevented timely compliance despite reasonable diligence. The registrar may require supporting material in original form whenever authenticity is material to the decision.

The ordinary remedy under appeals records and departmental determinations is prospective compliance, not retroactive substitution of a preferred academic outcome. Where a request is allowed, the student must identify the specific course, term, instructor, assessment, date, and regulation relied upon. General statements of hardship, disagreement with a mark, or dissatisfaction with advising are insufficient unless tied to a procedural right recognized by the handbook. Decisions must be reasoned, recorded, and retained for audit so that similarly situated students receive consistent treatment across schools, departments, and modes of delivery.

## 7.3 Departmental Waiver Determinations for Majors

For majors housed within a department, the Department Head possesses final, non-reviewable authority to grant prerequisite waivers when the Head determines that the student's preparation is substantially equivalent to the stated prerequisite.

The Department Head shall maintain a register of departmental waivers and shall provide a summary to the registrar at the end of each term. The register must identify the student, course, prerequisite, grounds for equivalency, and effective term, but the substantive decision is not reopened through the ordinary grievance route.
